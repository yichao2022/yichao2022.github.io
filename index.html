About Me

I hold a Ph.D. in Public Policy and Political Economy (Public Health, Behavioral Economics) from the University of Texas at Dallas.

My research lies at the intersection of Health Economics, Agentic AI, and Computational Behavioral Science. I specialize in developing and validating LLM-based agentic workflows for healthcare decision-making, combining Discrete Choice Experiments (DCEs), structural econometric modeling, and large-scale health data analysis.

I have extensive experience with EHR and claims data, having worked with linked administrative databases to model vaccination decisions and treatment effects. My recent work includes a 15-model LLM validation study (N=1,027) evaluating AI-generated behavioral responses against human choice data, developing frameworks to align generative agents with empirical choice frontiers.

My research has been supported by the UTD Dean of Graduate Education Dissertation Research Award and the NIJ Data Science for Criminal Justice Research Fellowship.

Health Economics & Pharmacoepidemiology

Waiting Time as a Behavioral Barrier to Vaccination Uptake: Nonlinear Heterogeneity by Institutional Trust Job Market Paper
Yichao Jin Job Market Paper.
Paper [PDF] Slides [PDF]
Examines waiting time as a behavioral barrier to preventive health decision-making using a Discrete Choice Experiment (N=1,027) in Wuhan, China. Formal interaction tests reveal that individuals with lower institutional trust are substantially more sensitive to delay. The study utilizes linked administrative data to model vaccination timing decisions and identify heterogeneity across urban-rural residency and education levels.
Ideological Contamination in Policy Simulation: Separating Political Bias from Model Capabilities
Working Paper.
Investigates the disentanglement of inherent LLM ideological priors from structural predictive capabilities. Proposes a bias-correction methodology to enhance the fidelity of administrative burden simulations.
